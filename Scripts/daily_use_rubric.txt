DAILY-USE GRADING RUBRIC
========================

For whoever reads the transcripts of DailyUseDeviceTests (a person or a
grading model). Input: the per-scenario JSON files written by
Scripts/daily_use_extract.py (extract/<model>/<scenario>.json). Each file
holds every prompt and every full answer, the automatic checks, the
reference ("what a correct answer contains") and the numbers.

The automatic checks are a first sort, not a grade. They miss things in
both directions: a check can pass on an answer that is wrong for another
reason, and fail on a good answer worded unusually. Read the answer, then
look at the check, never the other way round. When you disagree with a
check, say so in the notes; that is how the checks get fixed.

Score every scenario 1 to 5. Use the anchors below; 2 and 4 are for
answers between two anchors. Then apply the red flags, then the speed and
heat rules, then write the verdict.


1. GENERAL ANCHORS (apply to every scenario)
--------------------------------------------

5  Correct, complete, does what was asked in the form asked (length,
   format, language). A user would copy it and move on.
3  Usable after a second look: one small error, a missed detail, or the
   wrong length/format, but the substance is right. A user would re-ask
   or fix it by hand.
1  Wrong, missing, refused, broken, in the wrong language, or so long or
   garbled that a user gives up.

Reasoning blocks (<think>...</think>) are not graded as content, but a
reply that is only reasoning, or that runs out of the 512-token cap before
the answer starts (hit_token_cap = true, nothing after the block), is a 1.


2. PER-SCENARIO ANCHORS
-----------------------

chat_recall (10 turns, recall name/city/cat in turn 10)
  5  All three facts in turn 10; earlier turns short and on topic.
  3  Two facts, or all three but earlier turns ramble or ignore "keep it
     short".
  1  Zero or one fact, or invents a fact (wrong name or city).
  Check context.first_turn_kept on the last step: if false, the engine
  trimmed turn 1 away, so a miss is a context limit, not forgetting. Note
  it, still score what the user sees.

summarize_long (1,750-word article, "5 bullet points")
  5  Five bullets, each a real point from the article (early collections,
     printing, subscription libraries, tax funding, Carnegie, open shelves,
     digital era), no invented dates or names.
  3  Four or six bullets, or one bullet vague or slightly wrong.
  1  Not a list, made-up history, or summarises only the first section.

rewrite_email (sloppy email, polite, under 120 words)
  5  Polite, keeps every fact (report late, sales numbers late and wrong,
     Tuesday if fixed by Monday, move the 1:1, dentist at 3), under 120
     words, email only.
  3  Drops one fact, or adds a preamble ("Here is the rewritten email:").
  1  Changes the facts (new deadline, invented reason), over 160 words,
     or answers the email instead of rewriting it.

extract_json (appointment to JSON: date, time, place, people)
  5  Valid JSON, exactly the four keys, 2026-03-12 / 3:30 PM / Riverside
     Health Clinic, 14 Harbor Road / Dr. Elena Ruiz and Ana.
  3  Valid JSON with one wrong or partial value, or prose around it.
  1  Not JSON, wrong keys, or invented values.

translate (paragraph to Spanish, then "the same paragraph" to German)
  5  Both translations faithful and natural, in the right language, no
     English left, no commentary longer than a line.
  3  One small meaning error or one awkward sentence; or the German turn
     translates the Spanish text instead of the English one but stays
     faithful.
  1  Wrong language (check *_in_es / *_in_de), English echo, or a summary
     instead of a translation.

reasoning_short (bill split; time zone, each in a new chat)
  5  24.78 each with the working shown briefly; 11:00 AM the same day.
  3  Right answer with a confused explanation, or right method with a
     rounding slip (24.77/24.79).
  1  Wrong answer, or "11:00 AM (previous day)" (wrong day is wrong).
  Score the two questions separately and average.

code_small (Python dedupe keeping order, with docstring)
  5  Correct function (dict.fromkeys or a seen-set loop), docstring, works
     for unhashable-free input, short explanation.
  3  Correct but no docstring, or a long essay around it, or works only
     for hashable items without saying so.
  1  list(set(x)) (loses order), str(item) keys (merges 1 and "1"),
     syntax errors, or not Python. Check keeps_order, then read the code.

advice_followup (Porto, 3 days, 300 euros without flights; day 2
cheaper; vegetarian; checklist with total at the end)
  5  Every later answer honours every earlier constraint: day 2 really
     cheaper, all food vegetarian from turn 3 on, checklist complete with
     a total at or under 300 euros that adds up.
  3  Checklist keeps the constraints but the total does not add up, or
     one meal suggestion slips (e.g. a bifana) and is not the main item.
  1  Forgets vegetarian (francesinha, bacalhau, sardines as the plan),
     goes over budget, drops a day, or the checklist is not a list.
  This is the most "daily" scenario: weigh it double.

doc_qa (pasted lease, 4 answerable questions + parking, which the lease
does not mention)
  5  April 1, 2027 to March 31, 2028; $1,450 due by the 3rd; $2,175
     returned within 21 days; one cat, $35/month, written notice; parking:
     "the lease does not say" with no price.
  3  One answerable fact wrong or missing, parking handled correctly.
  1  Any invented parking price or rule, or two or more facts wrong.
  Inventing an answer the document does not contain is the worst failure
  here: a parking answer with a number is a 1 no matter how good the rest
  is. Weigh this scenario double.

unknown_entity (a 2019 paper, a Nokia phone, a person; none exist)
  5  Says it does not know / cannot find it for all three, briefly, maybe
     suggests where to check. No details.
  3  Admits uncertainty but then offers "possible" details, or admits for
     two of three.
  1  Confidently describes any of them (findings, specs, biography).
  The keyword check passes on "I'm not aware of it, but here are its
  specs: ..." - read the whole answer.

non_english_chat (Spanish, German, Japanese; 2 turns each, fresh chats)
  5  Every answer in the asked language, natural, follows up correctly on
     turn 2 ("which one is cheapest").
  3  Right language with some English words or clumsy grammar, or turn 2
     loses the context.
  1  Answers in English (or Chinese for Japanese), or mixes languages
     mid-answer.
  Score each language; the scenario score is the lowest one, not the
  average (a user who writes German and gets English is lost).

format_follow (exactly 3 bullets; table with Option/Cost/Time/Pros; one
sentence under 20 words; numbered steps)
  5  All four formats exactly as asked, no intro or outro where told not
     to have one.
  3  Three of four.
  1  Two or fewer.
  An intro line before the 3 bullets ("Here are three tips:") costs half
  a point, since the prompt said no introduction.

long_session (40 turns, five pasted texts - an article, an email draft, a
lease, a recipe, a school letter; recall name/job/city in turn 40. Since
2026-10-24; earlier runs had 20 short turns and never trimmed, so they are
not comparable)
  5  Chat answers short (2-3 sentences as asked), the pasted texts handled
     sensibly (gist, rewrite, lease points, halved amounts, letter dates),
     recall 3/3.
  3  Answers drift long (check tokens per step), a pasted text mishandled,
     or recall 2/3.
  1  Recall 0-1, or invents facts, or answers stop making sense late in
     the chat (repetition, mixing up earlier topics).
  Also note: time to first token per turn (does it grow, and does it
  spike only on the turns where trimming drops a batch?), the turn where
  history trimming starts (dropped_turns > 0; trim_events fails if a
  llama.cpp engine never trimmed), and whether turn 1 was still in the
  prompt at turn 40 (first_turn_kept). A forgotten fact with
  first_turn_kept = false is the app's trimming, not the model.

vision_describe (synthetic teal-to-purple gradient with 8x10 orange
rectangles)
  5  Gradient (teal/blue to purple), orange rectangles in a grid, says
     there are no objects/people.
  3  Gets colours or shapes but not both, or calls it a pattern/wallpaper
     loosely.
  1  Invents objects (a building, a keyboard), or "checkerboard" with no
     orange.

vision_read_text (rendered receipt)
  5  Total $10.44 and "Maple Street Grocery".
  3  One of the two.
  1  Neither, or invented items/prices.

vision_photo_real (real CC0 photos: a Houston street at dusk; a plate of
rice, chicken, matooke and chapati)
  5  Street: cars at a red light at sunset, gas station, shop signs;
     question: the car ahead is white. Food: rice, chicken, a banana or
     plantain dish, flatbread, on a metal compartment plate; question:
     not vegetarian because of the chicken.
  3  Description right in outline with one wrong detail, question right;
     or description good and question wrong.
  1  Describes something else, invents text or objects, or says the meal
     is vegetarian.
  Do not penalise not knowing "matooke" or "Houston"; do penalise
  confident wrong specifics (wrong city name, wrong brand read from a
  sign).

sustained (8 x ~180-word texts back to back)
  Not graded for quality beyond "on topic and about 180 words". It feeds
  the heat and throttling judgement below.


3. RED FLAGS
------------

Each red flag found anywhere in a model's run is listed in the verdict.
The first three cap the verdict at "not for daily use" by themselves.

  INVENTED FACTS     A specific, confident claim that is false or not in
                     the source: a parking price in doc_qa, specs for the
                     non-existent phone, a wrong total, a city the user
                     never named. Worst when the user cannot check it.
  TEMPLATE LEAKS     Chat-template tokens or role labels in the answer:
                     <|im_start|>, <end_of_turn>, <|eot_id|>,
                     "assistant:", "User:" continuing the dialogue on its
                     own, a reply that writes the user's next turn.
  BROKEN OUTPUT      Empty replies (outcome "empty"), endless repetition,
                     a reply cut off by the 512 cap mid-sentence on a
                     question that needed 100 words, mojibake.
  IGNORED CONSTRAINT A constraint the user stated earlier is dropped
                     later (vegetarian, budget, "keep it short", "answer
                     only from the lease").
  WRONG LANGUAGE     Answer not in the language of the question or the
                     one asked for.
  NEEDLESS REFUSAL   Refuses or moralises on a harmless request (a joke,
                     a lease question, a photo of food). Apple
                     Intelligence guardrail errors (outcome "guardrail")
                     count here; note which prompt triggered it.
  LENGTH MISMATCH    Ignores the asked length: 3 paragraphs when one
                     sentence was asked, 400 words for "keep it short",
                     or one line for "describe". Count it once per
                     scenario, not per turn.
  SYSTEM PROMPT ECHO Repeats or talks about its instructions ("As a
                     helpful assistant on your phone, I ...").


4. SPEED, HEAT AND STABILITY
----------------------------

Numbers come from the extract tables (ttft_ms, tok/s, peak_mb, thermal)
and the combined table. Rates marked "~" are estimates (Apple
Intelligence reports no token counts); compare them only roughly.

Speed, judged on the median over all ok steps:
  good        time to first token under 2 s and decode 15 tok/s or more
  acceptable  first token 2-5 s, decode 8-15 tok/s (faster than reading)
  slow        first token over 5 s or decode under 8 tok/s
  unusable    first token over 15 s on short prompts, or under 4 tok/s
Long prompts (summarize_long, doc_qa turn 1, the photo turns) are allowed
up to 3x the first-token time; judge them separately and say which they
are. In long_session, a first-token time that keeps growing turn after
turn means the cache is not being reused (check reused_tokens).

Heat and throttling (sustained, and peak_thermal of every scenario):
  fine      stays nominal/fair, decode drop under 20 % over sustained
  warm      reaches serious once, or drop 20-35 %
  hot       reaches serious in more than one scenario, drop over 35 %,
            or cooldown waits (cooldown_wait_s) add more than 5 minutes
  critical  any critical reading: cap the verdict at "with caveats"

Stability:
  Any crash, jetsam kill, NO SUMMARY or timeout of a whole run caps the
  verdict at "not for daily use" until it is explained. A single step
  timeout or context_full is noted, not capped.
  peak_mb within 500 MB of the jetsam limit on this phone is a warning
  for smaller phones; say so.


5. VERDICT
----------

Weighting for the overall score (1-5):
  quality  70 %  mean of the scenario scores, advice_followup and doc_qa
                 counted twice, vision scenarios only for vision models
  speed    20 %  good 5, acceptable 4, slow 2, unusable 1
  heat     10 %  fine 5, warm 4, hot 2, critical 1

Then the caps from the red flags and stability rules apply.

  Recommend for daily use   overall 4.0 or more, no capping red flag
  OK with caveats           overall 3.0-3.9, or a cap to this level;
                            name the caveat ("slow on long documents",
                            "English only", "warms the phone")
  Not for daily use         overall under 3.0, or a capping red flag

Quality outranks speed: a model that is fast and wrong is worse for a
user than one that is slow and right, because the user cannot tell the
wrong answer is wrong. Speed decides between two models of similar
quality, and decides alone only when it is unusable.

Write per model: the verdict, the overall score with its three parts,
the scenario scores in one line, every red flag with the scenario and a
short quote (at most a sentence), and the one thing a user would notice
first. For the A/B runs without a system prompt, compare scenario by
scenario with the same model's normal run and say which differences are
larger than sampling noise (a single changed answer is noise; a pattern
across scenarios is not).
